Jamestown Church Tower: then and now

1902 vs 2014 · Williamsburg and Jamestown, VA

The story

Jamestown's churches were rebuilt several times. Archaeologists believe six churches stood on two sites at James Fort. The first, inside the 1607 fort, burned on January 7, 1608.

In 1619 the first Representative Legislative Assembly met in the third church, on July 30. A brick church begun in 1639 burned during Bacon's Rebellion in 1676, and a fifth church was in use by about 1686 on its walls and foundations.

That church was abandoned in the 1750s and was in ruins by the 1790s, but its tower survived. It is the only 17th-century structure still standing at Jamestown. Preservation Virginia strengthened it in the 1890s, and in 1907 the Colonial Dames built a memorial church beside it, reusing the tower.

Did you know? The tower is the only 17th-century structure still standing at Jamestown.
Historians argue about this. Sources do not agree on when the tower went up. Wikipedia says it was added to the church in stages between 1639 (possibly earlier) and 1700. The state-marker text says the fifth church was built by 1686 on the fourth church's walls. This app does not pick one year. The site is run by Preservation Virginia with the Colonial Williamsburg Foundation, so check opening times and fees before you go.

Fact or tall tale?

  • SolidThe brick tower at Jamestown is the only 17th-century structure still standing there.
    The marker text says so, and Wikipedia describes the tower as the surviving part of the old church.
  • DisputedThe tower was built in 1686.
    The marker says the fifth church was built by 1686, but Wikipedia says the tower was added between 1639 and 1700. The exact date is uncertain.
