The story

A station first opened on this spot on June 1, 1881, after four railroads agreed to share one "union" station. A fire in 1894 destroyed the middle of it, and a bigger one went up in the Romanesque Revival style.

On July 4, 1906, a huge arch was dedicated in front of the station. It cost $22,500, used 70 tons of steel and had more than 2,000 light bulbs. One side said "Welcome" and the other said "Mizpah", a farewell word. The arch was torn down in 1931 because it was considered a traffic hazard.

The current central building, in the Beaux-Arts style, opened in 1914. After a big renovation, the station house reopened in summer 2014 with the 112-room Crawford Hotel, shops and a modern open-air train hall.

Did you know? The arch said "Welcome" on one side and "Mizpah" on the other.
Historians argue about this. Many people call the whole building the 1881 station. Wikipedia says the central part was replaced after the 1894 fire and again in 1914.
