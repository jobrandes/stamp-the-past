The story

The Fourth Ward School was built in 1876 in the Second Empire style, with a steep mansard roof and four stories. The architect was C. M. Bennett.

It was designed for more than 1,000 students. It had indoor plumbing, drinking fountains, gas lighting, and central heat. The last class graduated in 1936, and students moved to a new school.

The building sat closed and falling apart for years. In 1986 it reopened as the Historic Fourth Ward School Museum at 537 South C Street.

Did you know? One building held primary school, grammar school, and high school under the same roof.
Historians argue about this. Some pages call it the last wood-built school of its design in the United States. We could not find a source that backs that up.
