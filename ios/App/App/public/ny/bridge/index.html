The story

The bridge opened on May 24, 1883. It was first called the New York and Brooklyn Bridge, and got its current name in 1915. At opening it was the longest suspension bridge in the world, with a main span of 1,595.5 feet.

John A. Roebling designed it but died in 1869, before work began. His son Washington Roebling took over as chief engineer. Washington became too ill to visit the site, and his wife, Emily Warren Roebling, helped supervise the work for about 11 years.

Six days after opening, a stampede on the walkway killed at least twelve people. On May 17, 1884, P. T. Barnum led 21 elephants across to show the bridge was safe.

Did you know? Each stone tower is 278 feet tall. Four main cables each hold 5,282 galvanized steel wires.
Historians argue about this. Emily Roebling is officially listed as the first person to cross the bridge. Some accounts debate that, so this app treats it as a reported fact, not a certain one.

Fact or tall tale?

  • SolidThe Brooklyn Bridge opened in 1883.
    It opened on May 24, 1883, per Wikipedia.
  • Not trueThe bridge has always been called the Brooklyn Bridge.
    It was first the New York and Brooklyn Bridge. The current name became official in 1915.
