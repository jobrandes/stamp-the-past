The story

The statue was a gift from the people of France. The sculptor was Frédéric Auguste Bartholdi, and Gustave Eiffel's company built the iron frame inside. It was dedicated on October 28, 1886, with President Grover Cleveland.

The French people paid for the statue, and Americans paid for the pedestal. The statue was shiny reddish-brown copper at first. The green color came from oxidation and covered it by 1906.

The island was called Bedloe's Island until Congress renamed it in 1956. The famous poem, The New Colossus by Emma Lazarus, is on a plaque at the base.

Did you know? From the ground to the tip of the torch, the statue and pedestal together are about 305 feet tall.
Historians argue about this. Many people say Bartholdi's mother was the model for the face. The curator of the Bartholdi Museum says there is no factual basis for that, so we do not claim it.

Fact or tall tale?

  • SolidThe Statue of Liberty was dedicated in 1886.
    It was dedicated on October 28, 1886.
  • Not trueThe Statue of Liberty stands on Ellis Island.
    It stands on Liberty Island. Ellis Island is a separate island.
